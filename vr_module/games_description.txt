## Overview

The three VR serious games are designed as therapeutic tools that can be used both between teletherapy sessions and as interactive in-session environments through the platform. All three were developed in Unity using the Meta Extended Reality SDK, ensuring compatibility with Meta Quest headsets and consistent hand tracking, controller input, and spatial audio across games. Their design and therapeutic framing were developed alongside the input of real clinical therapists to ensure each game remains clinically sound and practically useful in a real therapy setting.

## Game One: Mindfulness and Positive Reflection

This game targets mood improvement and mindfulness through a calm, reflective item-collection mechanic set in a forest village. The patient collects items for a picnic, and each item triggers a short reflective prompt, such as recalling a positive event or naming something they are grateful for. The patient is guided to sit with that thought briefly before continuing, creating a gentle rhythm of activity and quiet reflection. This approach is useful in therapy because it helps patients redirect attention toward positive experiences in a low-pressure, non-confrontational way, making it accessible even to patients who may be hesitant toward more direct therapeutic exercises.

## Game Two: Breathing-Based Anxiety Regulation

This game targets anxiety regulation through real-time breathing-based biofeedback, set on a calm lake shore. The patient breathes using the headset controller as input, and as their breathing follows the guided rhythm, a storm on the lake gradually calms into clear skies. This direct link between the patient's breathing and the environment gives immediate, intuitive feedback, which reinforces the breathing technique without needing verbal instruction. It is useful in therapy because controlled breathing is one of the most common techniques for managing anxiety, and embedding it into an interactive, visually rewarding experience helps patients practice it more consistently on their own.

## Game Three: Collaborative Simulation and Crowd Exposure

This game supports live, therapist-guided exposure therapy through a synchronized multiplayer environment. The patient and therapist join the same virtual auditorium, where the therapist can adjust the size of the crowd and trigger their reactions in real time, ranging from supportive responses like applause to mild social stressors like distracted or distracting audience behavior. A neutral reset option lets the therapist instantly return the crowd to a calm state at any point. This game is useful in therapy because it gives therapists a safe, controllable way to expose patients to social and public-speaking triggers gradually, keeping the intensity of the exposure fully in the clinician's hands throughout the session.
